# Business Model Matrix v0.1

The Product Factory is a **multi-product, multi-channel distribution system**.

We may:
- build and own a product
- buy and resell an existing product where resale rights permit it
- white-label an existing capability
- bundle products/capabilities
- become an authorized distributor/reseller
- create a wrapper/orchestration layer around existing products
- manufacture a digital license/entitlement product ourselves

## Selection rule

The source of a product is secondary to:
1. lawful/contractual right to sell it
2. demand
3. margin
4. fulfillment reliability
5. customer support burden
6. channel availability
7. repeatability
8. defensibility or distribution advantage

## Product-source classes

### BUILD
We own the product/IP.

Advantages:
- highest control
- highest potential margin
- broadest packaging options

Risks:
- build time
- support
- discovery uncertainty

### BUY / RESELL
We purchase from a supplier and resell.

Advantages:
- fast launch
- lower development cost
- useful for testing demand

Requirements:
- legitimate supplier
- resale rights
- traceable invoices/records
- channel-compatible terms

Risks:
- supplier dependency
- margin compression
- authorization/compliance
- stock or entitlement failures

### WHITE-LABEL / LICENSE
A third party provides the underlying capability while we sell under our own commercial identity.

Requirements:
- explicit commercial rights
- clear support responsibility
- service continuity
- permitted branding/redistribution

### BUNDLE / COMPOSE
Combine several products/capabilities into a new commercial offer.

The bundle must create a meaningful customer outcome; it must not be a superficial repackaging.

## Portfolio principle

A healthy portfolio can contain all four classes simultaneously.

The objective is not to maximize self-built software.
The objective is to maximize **portfolio-level opportunity**:
- revenue potential
- margin
- distribution surface
- resilience
- learning rate
- option value
